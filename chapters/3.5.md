## 3.219

**Solved · high confidence.** Integrate the circular-loop Biot–Savart field.

**Assumptions**

- The loop lies in the xy plane with counterclockwise current from +z.

On the axis the transverse components cancel.

$$
\mathbf B(x)=\frac{\mu_0IR^2}{2(R^2+x^2)^{3/2}}\mathbf e_z.
$$

$$
B(0)=6.283185307\,\mu\mathrm T,\qquad B(R)=2.221441469\,\mu\mathrm T.
$$

**Results**

Centre field

$$
\mathbf B(0)=\frac{\mu_0I}{2R}\mathbf e_z
$$

Axial field at x=R

$$
\mathbf B(R)=\frac{\mu_0I}{2^{5/2}R}\mathbf e_z
$$

## 3.224

**Solved · high confidence.** Subtract the missing current strip from a complete cylindrical sheet.

**Assumptions**

- The tube is infinitely long, thin-walled and nonmagnetic; its remaining wall carries uniform current density along +z.
- The slit is centred on +x and has angular width delta approximately h/R. A filament approximation requires distance s from the slit line much greater than h.

The completed sheet with the same K has zero interior field.

$$
K=\frac{I}{R(2\pi-\delta)},\quad I_{\rm missing}=KR\delta\simeq\frac{Ih}{2\pi R}.
$$

Subtract the missing narrow filament at R*e_x.

$$
\mathbf s=\mathbf r-R\mathbf e_x,\quad \mathbf B(\mathbf r)\simeq-\frac{\mu_0Ih}{4\pi^2R}\frac{\mathbf e_z\times\mathbf s}{s^2}.
$$

At the centre the exact finite-slit result reduces to the leading narrow-slit expression.

$$
\mathbf B(0)=\frac{\mu_0I\sin(\delta/2)}{\pi R(2\pi-\delta)}\mathbf e_y\simeq\frac{\mu_0Ih}{4\pi^2R^2}\mathbf e_y.
$$

Near the slit keep its full angular distribution.

$$
\mathbf B(\mathbf r)=-\frac{\mu_0KR}{2\pi}\int_{-\delta/2}^{\delta/2}\frac{\mathbf e_z\times(\mathbf r-R\mathbf e_r(\theta))}{|\mathbf r-R\mathbf e_r(\theta)|^2}\,d\theta.
$$

**Results**

Interior field away from the slit

$$
\mathbf B\simeq-\frac{\mu_0Ih}{4\pi^2R}\frac{\mathbf e_z\times(\mathbf r-R\mathbf e_x)}{|\mathbf r-R\mathbf e_x|^2}
$$

Central field

$$
\mathbf B(0)\simeq\frac{\mu_0Ih}{4\pi^2R^2}\mathbf e_y
$$

**Limitations**

- Nonuniform conductivity or wall thickness would alter the assumed current distribution.

## 3.229

**Solved · high confidence.** Use sheet-current jumps and reflection symmetry.

**Assumptions**

- There is no imposed background field. The unit normal n points from the minus side to the plus side.
- For two sheets choose +i on the lower plane and -i on the upper plane, with n pointing upwards.

A single sheet has equal and opposite fields on its sides.

$$
\mathbf B_+=\frac{\mu_0}{2}(\mathbf i\times\mathbf n),\quad\mathbf B_-=-\frac{\mu_0}{2}(\mathbf i\times\mathbf n).
$$

Between opposite sheets the fields reinforce; outside they cancel.

$$
\mathbf B_{\rm between}=\mu_0(\mathbf i\times\mathbf n),\quad\mathbf B_{\rm outside}=0.
$$

**Results**

One current plane

$$
\mathbf B_\pm=\pm\frac{\mu_0}{2}(\mathbf i\times\mathbf n),\quad B=\mu_0i/2
$$

Opposite current planes

$$
\mathbf B_{\rm between}=\mu_0(\mathbf i\times\mathbf n),\quad\mathbf B_{\rm outside}=0
$$

## 3.230

**Solved · high confidence.** Integrate Ampere law across a uniform conducting slab.

**Assumptions**

- The slab occupies -d<=x<=d. Choose normal n=e_x and current j=j e_z; no external field is imposed.

Odd reflection symmetry gives B_y(0)=0; inside the field gradient equals mu0*j.

$$
\frac{dB_y}{dx}=\mu_0j,\quad\mathbf B=\mu_0x(\mathbf j\times\mathbf n).
$$

There is no current outside, so the field is constant in each exterior half-space and continuous at each surface.

$$
\mathbf B(x)=\begin{cases}\mu_0x(\mathbf j\times\mathbf n),&|x|\le d,\\\mu_0d\operatorname{sgn}(x)(\mathbf j\times\mathbf n),&|x|>d.\end{cases}
$$

**Results**

Slab field profile

$$
B(x)=\mu_0j\min(|x|,d),\quad\mathbf B(-x)=-\mathbf B(x)
$$

## 3.231

**Solved · high confidence.** Apply cylindrical symmetry to the wire and its complete radial return sheet.

**Assumptions**

- The conducting plane is z=0. Current descends along the positive z half-axis toward O and spreads radially outwards in the plane.
- No external magnetic field is imposed. Exclude the ideal axis, contact and sheet itself when quoting ordinary point values.

The complete current distribution gives an azimuthal field. Above the plane a circular path encloses -I; below it encloses no axial current.

$$
2\pi s B_\phi=\begin{cases}-\mu_0I,&z>0,\\0,&z<0.\end{cases}
$$

The radial current sheet accounts for the field jump.

$$
\mathbf K=\frac{I}{2\pi s}\mathbf e_s,\quad \mathbf e_z\times(\mathbf B_+-\mathbf B_-)=\mu_0\mathbf K.
$$

**Results**

Both half-space fields

$$
\mathbf B(s,z)=\begin{cases}-\dfrac{\mu_0I}{2\pi s}\mathbf e_\phi,&z>0,\\0,&z<0.\end{cases}
$$

## 3.232

**Solved · high confidence.** Integrate the loop axial field and interpret its circulation.

**Assumptions**

- Orient the axis coordinate x along the current-loop right-hand normal. R denotes the arbitrary loop radius.

The exact axial field integrates directly.

$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathbf B\cdot d\mathbf r=\frac{\mu_0I}{2}\left[\frac{x}{\sqrt{R^2+x^2}}\right]_{-\infty}^{\infty}=\mu_0I.
$$

Close the axis path at a very large distance. The return contribution tends to zero, and the resulting contour links the loop once.

$$
\oint\mathbf B\cdot d\mathbf r=\mu_0I_{\rm linked}=\mu_0I.
$$

**Results**

Axial circulation integral

$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathbf B\cdot d\mathbf r=\mu_0I
$$

## 3.233

## 3.234

**Solved · high confidence.** Subtract a negative-current cylinder representing the cavity.

**Assumptions**

- The material current density j is uniform and axial. The vector l points from the main wire axis to the cavity axis. The entire cavity lies within the wire.

For a point r in the cavity, add the full-cylinder field and the negative cavity-cylinder field.

$$
\mathbf B=\frac{\mu_0}{2}\mathbf j\times\mathbf r-\frac{\mu_0}{2}\mathbf j\times(\mathbf r-\mathbf l).

$$
\mathbf B=0
$$

## 3.235

**Solved · high confidence.** Differentiate the cylindrical magnetic circulation.

**Assumptions**

- Take B_phi=b*r^alpha, with positive circulation chosen about +z. The result is conventional current density; electrons move oppositely.
- The given radial symmetry and parallel flow exclude transverse current components.

Ampere law fixes the current within radius r.

$$
I(r)=\frac{2\pi rB_\phi}{\mu_0}=\frac{2\pi b}{\mu_0}r^{\alpha+1}.
$$

Differentiate the enclosed current with respect to cross-sectional area.

$$
j_z(r)=\frac{1}{2\pi r}\frac{dI}{dr}=\frac{b(\alpha+1)}{\mu_0}r^{\alpha-1}.
$$

**Results**

Conventional current density

$$
\mathbf j(r)=\frac{b(\alpha+1)}{\mu_0}r^{\alpha-1}\mathbf e_z
$$

**Limitations**

- The magnitude law alone does not determine the circulation sign; reversing that sign reverses j.

## 3.237

**Solved · high confidence.** Integrate circular turns from the end to infinity.

**Assumptions**

- Take x positive into the solenoid and x=0 at its end. The coil occupies the positive x half-axis and is sufficiently long to neglect its other end.
- The winding is represented by a uniform azimuthal sheet; the current right-hand normal points +x.

The continuous-loop integral from axial position s=0 to infinity has the same primitive as the finite coil.

$$
B_x(x)=\frac{\mu_0nIR^2}{2}\int_0^\infty\frac{ds}{[R^2+(x-s)^2]^{3/2}}=\frac{\mu_0nI}{2}\left(1+\frac{x}{\sqrt{R^2+x^2}}\right).
$$

The fractional deficit from the bulk field is eta; solve for the positive interior coordinate.

$$
\eta=\frac12\left(1-\frac{x_0}{\sqrt{R^2+x_0^2}}\right),\quad\frac{x_0}{R}=\frac{1-2\eta}{2\sqrt{\eta(1-\eta)}}=4.924685295.
$$

**Results**

Axial field and plot function

$$
\frac{B_x}{\mu_0nI}=\frac12\left(1+\frac{x/R}{\sqrt{1+(x/R)^2}}\right)
$$

One-percent distance

$$
x_0=\frac{1-2\eta}{2\sqrt{\eta(1-\eta)}}R\simeq4.92469R
$$

**Limitations**

- The semi-infinite approximation requires the other end to be far from the selected point.

## 3.238

**Solved · high confidence.** Resolve the helical sheet current into azimuthal and axial components.

**Assumptions**

- The conducting strip has insulated adjacent edges and a uniform longitudinal current across its width. There are no gaps between turns.
- Interpret h as the physical width perpendicular to the strip direction on the unrolled cylinder. Let beta be the helix angle relative to the azimuthal direction; positive current advances in both azimuth and +z.
- Neglect end effects and remote return-lead fields.

The unrolled pitch p and physical strip width satisfy h=p cos(beta); the helix gives tan(beta)=p/(2*pi*R).

$$
\sin\beta=\frac{h}{2\pi R},\quad p=\frac{h}{\sqrt{1-(h/2\pi R)^2}}.
$$

The sheet-current magnitude is I/h. Resolve it using the exact width geometry.

$$
K_\phi=\frac Ih\sqrt{1-(h/2\pi R)^2}=\frac Ip,\qquad K_z=\frac{I}{2\pi R}.
$$

The azimuthal current produces a uniform axial interior field; the axial current produces an exterior azimuthal field.

$$
\mathbf B(r)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{\mu_0I}{h}\sqrt{1-(h/2\pi R)^2}\mathbf e_z,&r<R,\\\dfrac{\mu_0I}{2\pi r}\mathbf e_\phi,&r>R.\end{cases}
$$

With the supplied dimensions the exact sheet interior field is slightly below the usual small-pitch value.

$$
B_{\rm in}=3.116023813\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm T,\quad B_{\rm out}=\frac{10^{-6}}{r\,[\mathrm m]}\ \mathrm T,\quad B_{\rm in}^{\rm small\ angle}\simeq\frac{\mu_0I}{h}=3.141592654\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm T.
$$

**Results**

Interior axial field

$$
\mathbf B=\frac{\mu_0I}{h}\sqrt{1-(h/2\pi R)^2}\mathbf e_z
$$

Exterior azimuthal field

$$
\mathbf B=\frac{\mu_0I}{2\pi r}\mathbf e_\phi=\frac{10^{-6}}{r\,[\mathrm m]}\mathrm T\,\mathbf e_\phi
$$

Usual width-as-pitch approximation

$$
B_{\rm in}\simeq\mu_0I/h=3.141592654\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm T
$$

**Limitations**

- The exterior field is not zero because a continuous helix transports net axial current. Nearby return leads can modify it.
- If h is instead defined as axial turn pitch, use K_phi=I/h directly. The leading small-angle expression is common to both width conventions.

## 3.239

**Solved · high confidence.** Separate the many poloidal turns from the net single azimuthal turn.

**Assumptions**

- The torus has very small minor radius relative to major radius R and a nonmagnetic wooden core.
- A single continuous wire advances once around the toroidal axis while making N poloidal turns. Lead and return fields are neglected.

The poloidal turns produce the dominant toroidal field inside the thin core.

$$
B_{\rm core}\simeq\frac{\mu_0NI}{2\pi R}.

The continuous winding also has one net azimuthal turn, whose field at the toroid centre is that of a loop of radius R.

$$
B_{\rm centre}\simeq\frac{\mu_0I}{2R},\quad \eta=\frac{B_{\rm core}}{B_{\rm centre}}\simeq\frac N\pi=795.7747155.
$$

**Results**

Core-to-centre field ratio

$$
\eta\simeq\frac N\pi\simeq7.96\times10^2
$$

**Limitations**

- The ratio is a thin-torus leading approximation. Lead routing can cancel or alter the small centre field.

## 3.240

**Solved · medium confidence.** Distinguish transverse cross-sectional flux from longitudinal internal flux.

**Assumptions**

- The straight round conductor is nonmagnetic and carries uniform axial current density.
- The literal transverse half-cross-section has normal along the wire. The conventional nonzero internal-flux interpretation uses a radial-longitudinal half-cut of length L=1 m, from the axis to the surface.

The internal field is azimuthal. It has zero component normal to an actual transverse cross-section.

$$
\mathbf B(r)=\frac{\mu_0Ir}{2\pi R^2}\mathbf e_\phi,\qquad \Phi_{\rm transverse}=\int\mathbf B\cdot\mathbf e_z\,dS=0.
$$

For the radial-longitudinal half-cut, integrate the normal azimuthal field from the axis to R.

$$
\Phi_{\rm radial}=L\int_0^R\frac{\mu_0Ir}{2\pi R^2}dr=\frac{\mu_0IL}{4\pi}=1.00\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm{Wb}\quad(L=1\ \mathrm m).
$$

**Results**

Literal transverse half-cross-section

$$
\Phi_{\rm transverse}=0
$$

Conventional internal half-cut flux per length

$$
\frac{\Phi_{\rm radial}}L=\frac{\mu_0I}{4\pi}=1.00\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm{Wb/m}
$$

**Limitations**

- The scan says half of the wire cross-section per metre of length; these geometrical phrases do not specify the same surface. Both interpretations are retained.

## 3.241

**Solved · high confidence.** Use reflection symmetry at the end of an ideal long solenoid.

**Assumptions**

- Interpret the requested end-plane flux as flux through the aperture of area S, with normal along the interior axial field.
- The winding is a uniform continuous current sheet and the distant end is negligible.

Reflect a semi-infinite solenoid in its end plane to form an infinite solenoid. Reflection makes the two normal field contributions equal everywhere on that plane.

$$
B_{n,\rm first}(r,0)=B_{n,\rm reflected}(r,0),\quad B_{n,\rm first}+B_{n,\rm reflected}=\mu_0nI\quad(r<R).
$$

Thus the normal field over the entire aperture, not just the axis, is half the bulk value.

$$
B_n(r,0)=\frac{\mu_0nI}{2}\quad(r<R),\qquad \Phi_{\rm aperture}=\frac{\mu_0nIS}{2}.
$$

**Results**

Magnetic flux through the end aperture

$$
\Phi_{\rm aperture}=\frac12\mu_0nIS
$$

**Limitations**

- For a finite solenoid, flux through the entire infinite end plane has zero net value once exterior return flux is included. The stated result is the aperture flux; distant-end and discrete-turn corrections are neglected.

## 3.242

**Solved · high confidence.** Integrate the toroidal field across its rectangular meridional cross section.

**Assumptions**

- Treat the densely and uniformly wound toroidal solenoid as ideal, with vacuum or nonmagnetic core. Ignore wire-pitch, discrete-turn and leakage-field corrections.
- Let a and b be the inner and outer radii. Their ratio b/a equals the stated outside-to-inside diameter ratio eta=1.6. The requested flux is through one core cross section, not N times this flux.

Ampere law determines the azimuthal field inside the core. A radial strip of the cross section has area h dr.

$$
B_\phi(r)=\frac{\mu_0NI}{2\pi r},\quad dS=h\,dr.
$$

Integrate from inner to outer radius and insert the data.

$$
\Phi=\int_a^bB_\phi h\,dr=\frac{\mu_0NIh}{2\pi}\ln\eta=7.990061697\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm{Wb}.
$$

**Results**

Flux through one toroidal cross section

$$
\Phi=\mu_0NIh\ln\eta/(2\pi)\simeq7.99006\ \mu\mathrm{Wb}
$$

## 3.243

**Solved · high confidence.** Eliminate loop current between its central field and magnetic dipole moment.

**Assumptions**

- Use a single circular thin loop in vacuum, and neglect any field from its feed wires.

The central field and current moment of the same loop contain the unknown current.

$$
B=\frac{\mu_0 I}{2R},\qquad \mathbf m=I\pi R^2\mathbf n.

$$
\mathbf m=\frac{2\pi R^3}{\mu_0}\mathbf B,\qquad m=0.0300\ \mathrm{A\,m^2}.
$$

**Results**

Magnetic moment magnitude

$$
m=2\pi BR^3/\mu_0=3.00\times10^{-2}\ \mathrm{A\,m^2}

## 3.285

**Solved · high confidence.** Integrate the induced-dipole force density along the long slender paramagnetic rod.

**Assumptions**

- B denotes the coil field without the small perturbation produced by the rod. Use weak linear paramagnetism, a long thin rod with negligible longitudinal demagnetizing correction and field approximately uniform over its cross section S.
- The coil current is maintained. One rod end is at the field maximum in the coil centre and the other is sufficiently far away that the imposed field is negligible.

To first order in intrinsic susceptibility, magnetization is J=chi B/mu0 and the induced-dipole force density contains one half of the B-squared gradient.

$$
f_x=\frac{\chi}{2\mu_0}\frac{d(B^2)}{dx}.
$$

Integrate over the rod volume, using only its two endpoint field values.

$$
F_x=\frac{\chi S}{2\mu_0}[B^2(x_2)-B^2(x_1)],\qquad\boxed{F=\frac{\chi SB^2}{2\mu_0}}\quad\text{toward the high-field coil centre}.
$$

**Results**

Attraction-force magnitude in the thin weak-paramagnet model

$$
F=\chi SB^2/(2\mu_0)
$$

Force direction

$$
\text{Pulls the rod farther into the coil}
$$

**Limitations**

- For appreciable susceptibility, finite rod demagnetization or a rod that substantially changes the coil field, a self-consistent geometry-dependent solution is needed.

## 3.286

**Solved · high confidence.** Include spherical demagnetization, then maximize the axial induced-dipole force.

**Assumptions**

- B(x) is the externally imposed electromagnet field before inserting the small sphere. Its variation over the sphere is negligible, and the source current is maintained.
- Use a homogeneous isotropic linear sphere with intrinsic material susceptibility chi defined by J=chi H_internal. The demagnetizing factor is 1/3; chi_eff=chi/(1+chi/3) relates its moment to external H0=B/mu0.
- The measured force is the magnetic attraction inferred by the balance after ordinary weight is accounted for. Take positive x away from the pole.

Solve the sphere response and write its force in the imposed field.

$$
p_m=\frac{V}{\mu_0}\frac{\chi}{1+\chi/3}B=\frac{V\chi_{\rm eff}}{\mu_0}B,\qquad F_x=\frac{V\chi_{\rm eff}}{2\mu_0}\frac{dB^2}{dx}=-\frac{2\alpha V\chi_{\rm eff}B_0^2}{\mu_0}xe^{-2\alpha x^2}.
$$

For x>0 the magnitude is proportional to x*exp(−2alpha*x²). Differentiate to find its unique positive maximum.

$$
\frac{d}{dx}(xe^{-2\alpha x^2})=e^{-2\alpha x^2}(1-4\alpha x^2),\qquad\boxed{x_m=\frac1{2\sqrt\alpha}=.0500\ {\rm m}}.
$$

Evaluate the maximum and infer the effective and intrinsic susceptibilities.

$$
F_{\max}=\frac{V\chi_{\rm eff}B_0^2\sqrt\alpha}{\mu_0\sqrt e},\qquad\chi_{\rm eff}=\frac{\mu_0\sqrt e\,F_{\max}}{VB_0^2\sqrt\alpha}=3.593442606\times10^{-4},\qquad\boxed{\chi=\frac{\chi_{\rm eff}}{1-\chi_{\rm eff}/3}=3.593873086\times10^{-4}}.
$$

**Results**

Height of maximum attraction

$$
x_m=5.00\ {\rm cm}
$$

Intrinsic volume susceptibility including spherical demagnetization

$$
\chi\simeq3.59387\times10^{-4}
$$

Leading weak-susceptibility estimate

$$
\chi\simeq\chi_{\rm eff}=3.59344\times10^{-4}
$$

**Limitations**

- For a nonspherical specimen the demagnetizing factor changes. A large sphere would require volume averaging of the nonuniform imposed field.

## 3.287

**Solved · high confidence.** Integrate the force of an induced spherical dipole, including its demagnetizing response.

**Assumptions**

- B is the imposed coil induction at the initial ball centre, with the small ball absent. The coil current is maintained and the field is locally uniform over the ball.
- The ball is a homogeneous isotropic linear paramagnet. Here chi is intrinsic material susceptibility defined by J=chi H_internal; spherical demagnetization gives chi_eff=chi/(1+chi/3).
- Slow displacement means quasistatic mechanical work without a change of kinetic energy. The requested positive work is that of the external agent pulling the paramagnet out; magnetic-force work has the opposite sign.

Self-consistency between the internal H and sphere demagnetizing field fixes its induced moment.

$$
H_{\rm in}=H_0-J/3,\quad J=\chi H_{\rm in},\qquad p_m=\frac{V\chi}{\mu_0(1+\chi/3)}B=\frac{V\chi_{\rm eff}}{\mu_0}B.
$$

Integrate the position-dependent induced dipole force. The magnetic mechanical potential includes the induced-response factor one half.

$$
F_x=\frac{V\chi_{\rm eff}}{2\mu_0}\frac{dB^2}{dx},\qquad U_{\rm mech}(B)=-\frac{V\chi_{\rm eff}B^2}{2\mu_0}.
$$

The final field is negligible, so external extraction work is the increase of this potential.

$$
\boxed{A_{\rm ext}=\frac{V\chi B^2}{2\mu_0(1+\chi/3)}};\qquad |\chi|\ll1:\quad A_{\rm ext}\simeq\frac{V\chi B^2}{2\mu_0},\qquad A_{\rm magnetic}=-A_{\rm ext}.
$$

**Results**

External quasistatic extraction work for intrinsic material susceptibility

$$
A_{\rm ext}=V\chi B^2/[2\mu_0(1+\chi/3)]
$$

Usual weak-paramagnet approximation

$$
A_{\rm ext}\simeq V\chi B^2/(2\mu_0)
$$

If supplied chi is already defined relative to external H0

$$
A_{\rm ext}=V\chi_{\rm external}B^2/(2\mu_0)
$$

Do not apply the demagnetizing factor a second time when susceptibility is defined against the imposed field.

**Limitations**

- Nonlinear susceptibility, hysteresis, a large ball or a coil field appreciably changed by the specimen requires additional constitutive/geometric information.
