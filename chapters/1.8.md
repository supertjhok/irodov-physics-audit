## 1.341

**Solved · high confidence.** Contract the components of each side parallel to the relative velocity, then sum the simultaneous side lengths.

**Assumptions**

- The triangle is an equilateral rigid shape at rest in its own frame. A moving-frame shape is a set of simultaneous endpoint positions, not an optical photograph.
- Write β=V/c and γ=(1−β²)^−1/2. Each rest-frame side component along V contracts by 1/γ; transverse components are unchanged.

For motion along a bisector, the opposite side is transverse and retains length a. Each remaining side has longitudinal component √3a/2 and transverse component a/2.

$$
P_a=a+2\sqrt{\frac{a^2}{4}+\frac{3a^2}{4\gamma^2}}=a\left[1+\sqrt{4-3\beta^2}\right].
$$

For motion along a side, that side contracts to a/γ. Each other side has longitudinal component a/2 and transverse component √3a/2.

$$
P_b=\frac a\gamma+2\sqrt{\frac{a^2}{4\gamma^2}+\frac{3a^2}{4}}=a\left[\sqrt{1-\beta^2}+\sqrt{4-\beta^2}\right].
$$

Expand each expression to leading nonrelativistic order.

$$
V\ll c:\qquad P_a=P_b=3a-\frac{3a}{4}\beta^2+O(\beta^4)=3a\left(1-\frac{\beta^2}{4}+O(\beta^4)\right).
$$

In the extreme limit the longitudinal dimension collapses. The remaining transverse extents determine the limiting perimeters.

$$
V\to c:\qquad P_a\to2a,\qquad P_b\to\sqrt3a.
$$

**Results**

Perimeter for motion along a bisector

$$
P_a=a[1+\sqrt{4-3(V/c)^2}]
$$

Perimeter for motion along a side

$$
P_b=a[\sqrt{1-(V/c)^2}+\sqrt{4-(V/c)^2}]
$$

Leading low-speed limits

$$
P_a=P_b=3a[1-V^2/(4c^2)+O(V^4/c^4)]
$$

Limits as V approaches c

$$
P_a\to2a,\qquad P_b\to\sqrt3a
$$

## 1.343

**Solved · medium confidence.** Apply axial length contraction to the cone height while leaving its transverse base radius fixed.

**Assumptions**

- Treat the stationary upright cone as a right circular cone. A simultaneous spatial shape in the moving frame is requested.
- Use “taper angle” primarily for the full apex angle θ; then its half-angle is α=θ/2. Because the English term can also denote an axis-to-generator half-angle, the alternative convention is given separately.

Let R be the base radius, H the proper height and L the proper generator length. Axial motion contracts H but leaves R unchanged.

$$
\tan\alpha=R/H,\qquad H^{\prime}=H/\gamma,\quad R^{\prime}=R,\qquad\tan\alpha^{\prime}=\gamma\tan\alpha.
$$

The moving-frame shape remains a right circular cone, so use its contracted height in the lateral-area formula.

$$
S_0=\pi R\sqrt{R^2+H^2},\qquad S=\pi R\sqrt{R^2+H^2/\gamma^2}=S_0\sqrt{\sin^2\alpha+\gamma^{-2}\cos^2\alpha}=S_0\sqrt{1-\beta^2\cos^2\alpha}.
$$

For the full apex angle θ=45°, use α=22.5° and γ=5/3.

$$
\theta^{\prime}=2\arctan\left[\frac53\tan22.5^\circ\right]=69.2390^\circ,\qquad S=4.0\sqrt{1-0.64\cos^222.5^\circ}=2.69437\ \mathrm{m^2}.

$$
\theta^{\prime}_{\rm half}=\arctan(5/3)=59.0362^\circ,\qquad S_{\rm half}=4.0\sqrt{1-0.64\cos^245^\circ}=3.29848\ \mathrm{m^2}.
$$

**Results**

Moving full apex angle, taking the given θ as full apex angle

$$
\theta^{\prime}=2\arctan[\gamma\tan(\theta/2)]\simeq69.2^\circ

Moving lateral area under the same full-angle convention

$$

If the given taper angle denotes the half-angle

$$

## 1.350

**Solved · high confidence.** Use the contracted length of the moving rod and the separation between corresponding-end coincidence events.

**Assumptions**

- Work in the rest frame of either rod and let u be the speed of the other. Interpret the events as right-end with right-end and left-end with left-end coincidences.
- Let γu=(1−u²/c²)^−1/2. The physically relevant interval for these corresponding-end coincidences satisfies 0<Δt<l0/c.

In this frame one rod has length l0 and the other l0/γu. Between the two corresponding-end coincidences the moving rod translates by their length difference.

$$
u\Delta t=l_0-\frac{l_0}{\gamma_u}=l_0\left(1-\sqrt{1-u^2/c^2}\right).
$$

Square and solve for the nonzero speed, retaining the sign condition of the original square root.

$$
u^2\left(\frac1{c^2}+\frac{\Delta t^2}{l_0^2}\right)=\frac{2u\Delta t}{l_0},\qquad u=\frac{2l_0c^2\Delta t}{l_0^2+c^2\Delta t^2}.
$$

A dimensionless form exhibits the physical branch and the speed bound.

$$
q=\frac{c\Delta t}{l_0},\qquad\frac uc=\frac{2q}{1+q^2},\qquad0<q<1.
$$

**Results**

Relative rod speed

$$
u=\frac{2l_0c^2\Delta t}{l_0^2+c^2\Delta t^2},\qquad0<\Delta t<l_0/c
$$

**Limitations**

- An interval from first contact of opposite ends to final separation is a different event pair and has Δt=(l0+l0/γu)/u>l0/c. Its squared algebra can produce the same rational expression, but its physical domain and event interpretation differ.

## 1.356

**Solved · high confidence.** Use the causal-separation bound to show that a subluminal Lorentz boost cannot reverse the time order of causally related events.

**Assumptions**

- Let event 2 be a distinct effect of event 1, so Δt>0 and the connecting influence propagates no faster than light. In K this implies |Δr|≤cΔt.
- Compare inertial frames with the same future time orientation; rotations and translations do not reverse causal ordering.

Write the time transformation for an arbitrary boost velocity V, allowing a three-dimensional separation vector.

$$
\Delta t^{\prime}=\gamma\left(\Delta t-\frac{\mathbf V\cdot\Delta\mathbf r}{c^2}\right).
$$

Bound the boost term using Cauchy’s inequality and the causal speed limit.

$$
\mathbf V\cdot\Delta\mathbf r\leq V|\Delta\mathbf r|\leq Vc\Delta t.
$$

For every physical relative speed V<c the resulting lower bound is strictly positive.

$$
\Delta t^{\prime}\geq\gamma\Delta t\left(1-\frac Vc\right)>0.
$$

Equivalently the separation is future timelike or future null, and the invariant interval cannot become spacelike under a Lorentz transformation.

$$
c^2\Delta t^2-|\Delta\mathbf r|^2\geq0,\qquad \Delta t>0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\Delta t^{\prime}>0.
$$

**Results**

Causal order preserved in every future-oriented inertial frame

$$
\Delta t^{\prime}\geq\gamma\Delta t(1-V/c)>0
$$

The cause remains earlier than the effect for both slower-than-light and lightlike causal propagation.
