## 6.291

**Solved · high confidence.** Invert the relativistic energy–momentum relation for each input momentum.

**Assumptions**

- The proton rest energy is 938.182 MeV from the Appendix hydrogen-1 mass minus one electron rest energy. Atomic binding is negligible here.
- The three quoted momenta are laboratory momenta.

Write the total energy in terms of rest energy and momentum, then subtract the rest energy.

$$
T=\sqrt{(pc)^2+(m_pc^2)^2}-m_pc^2=\frac{(pc)^2}{\sqrt{(pc)^2+(m_pc^2)^2}+m_pc^2}.
$$

Use the same energy units for all three momentum–energy products.

$$
pc=(100,1000,10000)\ \mathrm{MeV},\qquad T\simeq(5.3144,433.017,9105.73)\ \mathrm{MeV}.
$$

**Results**

At 0.10 GeV/c

$$
T\simeq5.31\ \mathrm{MeV}
$$

At 1.0 GeV/c

$$
T\simeq0.433\ \mathrm{GeV}
$$

At 10 GeV/c

$$
T\simeq9.11\ \mathrm{GeV}
$$

## 6.292

**Solved · high confidence.** Convert the proper mean lifetime to a laboratory mean path.

**Assumptions**

- Interpret the stated factor as T/(m_π c²)=1.2. The pions travel freely at fixed speed.

The Lorentz factor is the total energy divided by the rest energy. The laboratory mean time is γ times the proper mean time.

$$
\gamma=1+\eta,\qquad \overline{\ell}=\beta\gamma c\tau_0.
$$

Eliminate the speed using the Lorentz identity and insert the stated lifetime.

$$
\beta\gamma=\sqrt{\gamma^2-1}=\sqrt{\eta(2+\eta)},\qquad \overline{\ell}=c(25.5\ \mathrm{ns})\sqrt{1.2(3.2)}.
$$

**Results**

Mean laboratory path

$$
\overline{\ell}\simeq15.0\ \mathrm m
$$

## 6.293

**Solved · high confidence.** Invert the boosted mean path to obtain the proper lifetime.

**Assumptions**

- The pions have 100 MeV kinetic energy in the laboratory and retain that energy until decay.

Compute the dimensionless boost from the charged-pion rest energy and kinetic energy.

$$
\beta\gamma=\frac{pc}{m_\pi c^2}=\frac{\sqrt{T(T+2m_\pi c^2)}}{m_\pi c^2}\simeq1.395.
$$

The distance is the laboratory speed multiplied by the dilated mean lifetime.

$$
\tau_0=\frac{\overline{\ell}}{c\beta\gamma}\simeq26.30\ \mathrm{ns}.
$$

**Results**

Proper mean lifetime

$$
\tau_0\simeq26\ \mathrm{ns}
$$

## 6.294

**Assumptions**

Use T=m_π c² to obtain γ=2, then convert the proper mean lifetime into the laboratory mean decay length.

$$
\beta\gamma=\sqrt{3},\qquad \ell_d=\sqrt{3}\,c\tau_0\simeq13.24\ \mathrm m.

The proper flight time between sections is their separation divided by βγc.

$$
\frac{\Phi_{\rm down}}{\Phi_{\rm up}}=\exp\!\left(-\frac{20\ \mathrm m}{\ell_d}\right).
$$

**Results**

Downstream flux divided by upstream flux

$$
\Phi_{\rm down}/\Phi_{\rm up}\simeq0.221
$$

Reciprocal flux ratio

$$
\Phi_{\rm up}/\Phi_{\rm down}\simeq4.53
$$

## 6.295

**Solved · high confidence.** Use the two-body decay invariant in the charged-pion rest frame.

**Assumptions**

- The notation M_i denotes rest energy m_i c², and q denotes momentum times c.
- The neutrino mass is negligible on the MeV scale. The physical positive-pion mode emits a positive muon and a muon neutrino.

The two particles have equal and opposite momenta. For the neutrino, energy equals momentum times c.

$$
M_\pi=\sqrt{M_\mu^2+E_\nu^2}+E_\nu.
$$

Solve for the neutrino energy, then subtract the muon rest energy from the muon total energy.

$$
E_\nu=\frac{M_\pi^2-M_\mu^2}{2M_\pi},\qquad T_\mu=M_\pi-E_\nu-M_\mu.
$$

**Results**

Muon kinetic energy

$$
T_\mu\simeq4.120\ \mathrm{MeV}
$$

Neutrino energy

$$
E_\nu\simeq29.792\ \mathrm{MeV}
$$

## 6.296

**Solved · high confidence.** Use the neutron energy in a two-body decay of a stationary parent.

**Assumptions**

- The notation M_i denotes rest energy m_i c², and q denotes momentum times c.
- The Appendix neutron mass in atomic mass units corresponds to a rest energy of 939.475 MeV with the printed conversion.

Square the pion four-momentum, expressed as the parent four-momentum minus the neutron four-momentum.

$$
M_\pi^2=M_\Sigma^2+M_n^2-2M_\Sigma E_n.
$$

Subtract the neutron rest energy to find the kinetic energy.

$$
T_n=\frac{(M_\Sigma-M_n)^2-M_\pi^2}{2M_\Sigma}.
$$

**Results**

Neutron kinetic energy

$$
T_n\simeq19.65\ \mathrm{MeV}
$$

## 6.297

**Solved · high confidence.** Minimize the invariant mass of the two-neutrino recoil system.

**Assumptions**

- The notation M_i denotes rest energy m_i c², and q denotes momentum times c.
- The muon is at rest. Neutrino masses are negligible for the endpoint.

Group the two neutrinos into a single recoil system of invariant mass-energy W. If the two massless neutrinos are collinear, W can reach zero.

$$
W^2=2E_{\nu_1}E_{\nu_2}(1-\cos\theta_{12})\ge0,\qquad E_{e^+}=\frac{M_\mu^2+M_e^2-W^2}{2M_\mu}.
$$

The positron energy decreases with W². Set W to its minimum and remove the positron rest energy.

$$
T_{\max}=\frac{M_\mu^2+M_e^2}{2M_\mu}-M_e=\frac{(M_\mu-M_e)^2}{2M_\mu}.
$$

**Results**

Positron kinetic-energy endpoint

$$
T_{\max}\simeq52.32\ \mathrm{MeV}
$$

## 6.298

**Solved · medium confidence.** Reconstruct the parent mass from the back-to-back proton and pion energies.

**Assumptions**

- The notation M_i denotes rest energy m_i c², and q denotes momentum times c.
- The proton rest energy is 938.182 MeV from the Appendix hydrogen-1 mass minus one electron rest energy. Atomic binding is negligible here.
- The parent is at rest and the two stated particles are its full decay products.

Infer the proton momentum from its kinetic energy. The pion has the same momentum magnitude.

$$
q^2=T_p(T_p+2M_p),\qquad E_p=M_p+T_p,\qquad E_\pi=\sqrt{M_\pi^2+q^2}.
$$

In the parent rest frame the sum of daughter energies is the parent rest energy.

$$
M_X=M_p+T_p+\sqrt{M_\pi^2+T_p(T_p+2M_p)}\simeq1115.10\ \mathrm{MeV}.
$$

The neutral Lambda has a tabulated rest energy of 1115.683 MeV and a proton–negative-pion decay channel. The reconstructed rest energy is about 0.58 MeV lower.

$$
\Lambda^0\to p+\pi^-.
$$

**Results**

Mass reconstructed from the stated proton energy

$$
m_X\simeq1.115\times10^3\ \mathrm{MeV}/c^2
$$

Particle identification

$$
X=\Lambda^0
$$

**Limitations**

- The exact reconstruction and the tabulated Lambda mass are both retained.

## 6.299

**Solved · high confidence.** Find the neutrino energy from the recoil muon invariant at a right angle.

**Assumptions**

- The notation M_i denotes rest energy m_i c², and q denotes momentum times c.
- The negative-pion channel emits a muon antineutrino, whose mass is negligible here. The 90° angle is in the laboratory.

The daughter muon four-momentum is the pion four-momentum minus the neutrino four-momentum. Squaring it gives an equation linear in the neutrino energy.

$$
M_\mu^2=M_\pi^2-2E_\nu\bigl(E_\pi-q_\pi\cos\theta\bigr).
$$

The angular term vanishes at 90°. The pion total energy is its rest energy plus 50 MeV.

$$
E_\nu=\frac{M_\pi^2-M_\mu^2}{2(M_\pi+50\ \mathrm{MeV})}.
$$

**Results**

Neutrino energy at 90° in the laboratory

$$
E_\nu\simeq21.9\ \mathrm{MeV}
$$

## 6.300

**Solved · high confidence.** Find the invariant mass of the unseen particle from the two measured four-momenta.

**Assumptions**

- The notation M_i denotes rest energy m_i c², and q denotes momentum times c.
- The parent and emitted pion kinetic energies and their right angle are laboratory quantities.

Subtract the outgoing pion four-momentum from the incoming hyperon four-momentum. The three-momentum cross term vanishes at a right angle.

$$
M_X^2=M_\Sigma^2+M_\pi^2-2(E_\Sigma E_\pi-\mathbf q_\Sigma\cdot\mathbf q_\pi)=M_\Sigma^2+M_\pi^2-2E_\Sigma E_\pi.
$$

Use total energies including the two respective rest energies.

$$
M_X=\sqrt{M_\Sigma^2+M_\pi^2-2(M_\Sigma+320)(M_\pi+42)}\simeq941.26\ \mathrm{MeV}.
$$

**Results**

Missing mass in MeV/c²

$$
m_X\simeq9.4\times10^2\ \mathrm{MeV}/c^2
$$

**Limitations**

- The reconstructed value is about 1.78 MeV above the Appendix neutron rest energy. The Sigma-baryon table lists the neutron–positive-pion decay mode. The calculation uses the supplied kinematics as given.

## 6.301

**Solved · high confidence.** Determine the parent invariant from two equal-energy photon four-momenta.

**Assumptions**

- The notation M_i denotes rest energy m_i c², and q denotes momentum times c.
- The photons have equal energies in the laboratory.

For massless photons, the invariant of their four-momentum sum depends only on their energies and opening angle.

$$
M_{\pi^0}^2=2E_\gamma^2(1-\cos\Theta)=4E_\gamma^2\sin^2(\Theta/2).
$$

At 60° the sine of the half-angle is one half. The parent kinetic energy is the total photon energy minus the parent rest energy.

$$
E_\gamma=M_{\pi^0},\qquad T_{\pi^0}=2E_\gamma-M_{\pi^0}=M_{\pi^0}.
$$

**Results**

Neutral-pion kinetic energy

$$
T_{\pi^0}\simeq135.0\ \mathrm{MeV}
$$

Energy of each photon

$$
E_\gamma\simeq135.0\ \mathrm{MeV}
$$

## 6.302

**Solved · high confidence.** Equate the initial invariant to the minimum final invariant mass.

**Assumptions**

- The target is stationary. The stated outgoing rest masses include all required products. The reaction is endoergic and the threshold is kinematic.

The incident particle contributes its rest energy plus its laboratory kinetic energy. Expanding the total invariant with the target at rest gives a term linear in the incident kinetic energy.

$$
s=(E_m+Mc^2)^2-p_m^2c^2=(m+M)^2c^4+2Mc^2T.
$$

The final invariant is the square of the total energy in the center-of-momentum frame. At threshold all massive products share its velocity in the laboratory.

$$
s_{\rm th}=c^4\left(\sum_i m_i\right)^2.
$$

Solve the initial invariant equation at that minimum.

$$
T_{\rm th}=\frac{\left(\sum_i m_i\right)^2-(m+M)^2}{2M}\,c^2.
$$

**Results**

Laboratory threshold, Eq. (6.7c)

$$
T_{\rm th}=\frac{\left(\sum_i m_i\right)^2-(m+M)^2}{2M}c^2
$$

**Limitations**

- The expression gives the kinematic limit; reaction rates or a required massless product can affect attainment of the limit.

## 6.303

**Solved · high confidence.** Use the equal-photon momentum bisector and conserve laboratory energy and momentum.

**Assumptions**

- The notation M_i denotes rest energy m_i c², and q denotes momentum times c.
- The electron is free and stationary. Both photons have equal laboratory energy.

The initial total energy is the incident kinetic energy plus the two rest energies. The incident positron carries the full initial momentum.

$$
E_{\rm tot}=T+2M_e=2E_\gamma,\qquad q_{\rm in}=\sqrt{T(T+2M_e)}.
$$

The equal photon momenta add along their bisector. Their component along it is the photon momentum times the cosine of the half-angle.

$$
q_{\rm in}=2E_\gamma\cos(\Theta/2),\qquad \Theta=2\arccos\sqrt{\frac{T}{T+2M_e}}.
$$

**Results**

Photon opening angle

$$
\Theta\simeq98.8^\circ
$$

## 6.304

**Solved · high confidence.** Apply the photon–target threshold invariant with the recoil target included.

**Assumptions**

- The notation M_i denotes rest energy m_i c², and q denotes momentum times c.
- The proton rest energy is 938.182 MeV from the Appendix hydrogen-1 mass minus one electron rest energy. Atomic binding is negligible here.
- The targets are free and at rest. The final rest-energy sums include the original target particle.

The photon is massless. Combine its four-momentum with the stationary target four-momentum, then impose zero relative product momentum in the center-of-momentum frame.

$$
s=M_{\rm target}^2+2M_{\rm target}E_\gamma,\qquad E_{\gamma,\rm th}=\frac{W_f^2-M_{\rm target}^2}{2M_{\rm target}}.
$$

For an electron target the final rest-energy sum is three electron masses. For a proton target, add the rest energies of two charged pions to the proton rest energy.

$$
E_{\gamma,a}=\frac{(3M_e)^2-M_e^2}{2M_e}=4M_e,\qquad E_{\gamma,b}=2M_\pi+\frac{2M_\pi^2}{M_p}.
$$

**Results**

Electron–positron pair on an electron

$$
E_{\gamma,\rm th}=2.044\ \mathrm{MeV}
$$

Charged-pion pair on a proton

$$
E_{\gamma,\rm th}\simeq320.7\ \mathrm{MeV}
$$

## 6.305

**Assumptions**

- The proton rest energy is 938.182 MeV from the Appendix hydrogen-1 mass minus one electron rest energy. Atomic binding is negligible here.
- Treat the target hydrogen nucleus as a stationary proton and neglect the atomic electron.

The invariant for a beam proton of kinetic energy T incident on a stationary proton is linear in T.

$$
s=4M_p^2+2M_pT,\qquad T_{\rm th}=\frac{W_f^2-4M_p^2}{2M_p}.

Use four equal nucleon rest energies in part (a). In part (b), add the neutral-pion rest energy to the two proton rest energies.

$$
W_{f,a}=4M_p,\qquad W_{f,b}=2M_p+M_{\pi^0},\qquad T_{\rm th,a}=6M_p,\quad T_{\rm th,b}=2M_{\pi^0}+\frac{M_{\pi^0}^2}{2M_p}.

**Results**

Antiproton-production threshold

Neutral-pion production threshold

## 6.307

**Solved · medium confidence.** Use the Gell-Mann–Nishijima relation to solve for hypercharge and strangeness.

**Assumptions**

- Use the light-hadron relation in Eq. (6.7d) with charge in units of e. Identify the usual ground-state baryon.

At zero charge and positive half-unit of isospin projection, the hypercharge term cancels the isospin term.

$$
0=\frac12+\frac Y2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad Y=-1,\qquad S=Y-B=-2.
$$

The ground-state cascade doublet has B=1 and S=−2. Its member with positive isospin projection is neutral.

$$
\Xi^0=uss,\qquad Q=\tfrac23-\tfrac13-\tfrac13=0.
$$

**Results**

Strangeness

$$
S=-2
$$

Hypercharge

$$
Y=-1
$$

Ground-state identification

$$
\Xi^0
$$

**Limitations**

- Excited cascade baryons can share these additive quantum numbers.

## 6.308

**Solved · high confidence.** Count total lepton number on each side of every process.

**Assumptions**

- Each lepton has L=+1, each antilepton L=−1, and hadrons have L=0. The scan uses a tilde to denote an antineutrino.

In (1) the electron and neutrino each contribute +1. In (2) the electron–positron pair cancels, leaving the positive muon contribution of −1. In (3) the negative muon and neutrino each contribute +1.

$$
(L_i\to L_f)_{1,2,3}=(0\to2,\ 0\to-1,\ 0\to2).
$$

In (4) the incoming electron and the emitted neutrino each give +1. In (5) the neutrino pair has zero total lepton number. In (6) the antineutrino cancels the muon lepton number.

$$
(L_i\to L_f)_{4,5,6}=(1\to1,\ -1\to-1,\ 0\to0).
$$

The usual flavor assignments for the three conserving cases are electron neutrino, electron neutrino plus muon antineutrino, and muon antineutrino, respectively.

$$
(4)\ \nu_e;\qquad (5)\ \nu_e+\bar\nu_\mu;\qquad (6)\ \bar\nu_\mu.
$$

**Results**

Forbidden by total lepton charge

$$
\{1,2,3\}
$$

Conserve total lepton charge

$$
\{4,5,6\}
$$

**Limitations**

- The classification tests only lepton charge. For example, free-proton capture in (4) also has an energetic threshold.

## 6.310

**Solved · high confidence.** Identify a failed kinematic or conservation condition in each listed process.

**Assumptions**

- Use the printed particle and antiparticle labels. Apply strangeness conservation to the strong channel and separate electron- and muon-flavor counting to the ordinary charged-current muon decay.

The stationary Sigma-minus has insufficient invariant mass for a Lambda and a charged pion, even with both outgoing particles at their minimum total energy.

$$
(1)\qquad (m_\Lambda+m_{\pi^-}-m_{\Sigma^-})c^2\simeq57.804\ \mathrm{MeV}>0.
$$

The proton in (2) contributes one unit of baryon number and both kaons have zero. In (3) the incident kaon supplies negative charge, whereas the outgoing omega and positive kaon charges cancel.

$$
(2)\quad B:1\to0,\qquad (3)\quad Q/e:-1\to0.
$$

Two nonstrange nucleons become two hyperons of strangeness −1 in (4). The electron–positron pair in (5) cancels in total lepton number, leaving the negative muon.

$$
(4)\quad S:0\to-2,\qquad (5)\quad L:0\to1.
$$

The last neutrino in (6) carries the printed antineutrino tilde. The outgoing electron and electron neutrino give two units of electron-flavor number. The muon antineutrino gives −1 unit of muon-flavor number. The usual charged-current mode emits an electron antineutrino and a muon neutrino.

$$
(6)\quad(L_e,L_\mu):(0,+1)\to(+2,-1),\qquad\mu^-\to e^-+\bar\nu_e+\nu_\mu\quad\text{(usual decay)}.
$$

**Results**

Insufficient parent rest energy

$$
(m_\Lambda+m_{\pi^-}-m_{\Sigma^-})c^2\simeq57.8\ \mathrm{MeV}
$$

Baryon number changes

$$
B_i=1,\ B_f=0
$$

Electric charge changes

$$
Q_i/e=-1,\ Q_f/e=0
$$

Strangeness changes in the proposed strong channel

$$
S_i=0,\ S_f=-2
$$

Total lepton charge changes

$$
L_i=0,\ L_f=1
$$

Lepton-family assignments disagree with ordinary muon decay

$$
(L_e,L_\mu)_i=(0,+1),\ (L_e,L_\mu)_f=(+2,-1)
$$

**Limitations**

- The rule in (4) refers to strong interactions. The flavor count in (6) is the ordinary charged-current production assignment.
