## 3.148

**Assumptions**

$$

$$

## 3.153

## 3.156

**Assumptions**

## 3.158

**Assumptions**

- Put the plane at z=0 and the ball centre at z=l. The radius a is much smaller than l. The ball voltage relative to the plane is V.
- The stated r is the distance from the ball centre to the observation point on the plane. If s is planar radial distance, r=sqrt(s*s+l*l) and r>=l.
- The medium has uniform resistivity rho. Use a spherical monopole and its reflected sink; neglect higher induced multipoles, contact resistance and electrode resistance.

The conduction source/sink potential has the same geometry as an electrostatic charge and its opposite plane image.

$$
\varphi(\mathbf r)=\frac{\rho I}{4\pi}\left(\frac1{|\mathbf r-l\mathbf e_z|}-\frac1{|\mathbf r+l\mathbf e_z|}\right),\quad V\simeq\frac{\rho I}{4\pi}\left(\frac1a-\frac1{2l}\right).
$$

$$
\mathbf j(s,0^+)=-\frac{Il}{2\pi(s^2+l^2)^{3/2}}\mathbf e_z,\qquad j(r)=\frac{2aVl}{\rho r^3[1-a/(2l)]}\simeq\frac{2aVl}{\rho r^3}.

$$
R\simeq\frac{\rho}{4\pi a}\left(1-\frac a{2l}\right)\sim\frac{\rho}{4\pi a}.

$$
j(r)\simeq2a|V|l/(\rho r^3),\quad r\ge l

For positive V, current enters the plane normally. Divide the leading expression by 1-a/(2*l) to retain the displayed monopole correction.

$$
R\simeq\rho/(4\pi a)

The first monopole correction multiplies the leading resistance by 1-a/(2*l).

## 3.159

**Solved · high confidence.** Use opposite infinite line-current sources and reflection symmetry at an equidistant observation point.

**Assumptions**

- The wires are ideal electrodes at potential difference V. Their radius a is much smaller than their axis separation l, and end effects are neglected.
- Let i be leakage current per unit wire length. Different axial segments conduct transversely through the medium in parallel.
- The observation point has equal distance r>=l/2 from both axes. Its current density points from the positive wire toward the negative wire.

A line source has radial current density i/(2*pi*r). Integrate its electric field, rho*j, and combine the source and sink logarithmic potentials.

$$
V\simeq\frac{\rho i}{\pi}\ln(l/a),\qquad i\simeq\frac{\pi V}{\rho\ln(l/a)}.
$$

At an equidistant point, the two displacement vectors have the same denominator. Their difference is the interaxis displacement of magnitude l.

$$
j(r)=\frac{il}{2\pi r^2}\simeq\frac{|V|l}{2\rho r^2\ln(l/a)}.
$$

For a wire length L, the total leakage current is i*L. State the resistance-length product rather than treating transverse conduction as longitudinal resistance.

$$
R(L)=\frac{V}{iL}\simeq\frac{\rho\ln(l/a)}{\pi L},\qquad R(L)L=\frac{\rho}{\pi}\ln(l/a),\quad\frac{G}{L}=\frac\pi{\rho\ln(l/a)}.
$$

**Results**

Current density at an equidistant point

$$
j(r)\simeq |V|l/[2\rho r^2\ln(l/a)]
$$

Transverse resistance-length product

$$
R(L)L\simeq\rho\ln(l/a)/\pi
$$

For a 1-metre wire pair, divide this coefficient by 1 metre to obtain resistance in ohms. The resistance scales as1/L; R/L is not a length-independent coefficient for transverse leakage.

## 3.162

**Solved · high confidence.** Combine steady insulating-boundary charge continuity with tangential electric-field continuity and the normal displacement jump.

**Assumptions**

- Interpret D as the displacement immediately outside the conductor in the linear dielectric of relative permittivity epsilon. The unit normal points from conductor to dielectric, and the unit tangent follows the tangential component of D.
- Use the dc steady state, with no accumulating surface charge and an ideally insulating exterior dielectric. Its normal conduction current is zero.
- The conductor obeys local Ohm law with resistivity rho. Assume no contact emf or singular tangential field at the interface.

Steady charge conservation makes the conductor normal current zero. Its normal electric field therefore vanishes.

$$
\partial_t\sigma_f=j_{n,\rm con}-j_{n,\rm diel}=0,\quad j_{n,\rm diel}=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad E_{n,\rm con}=0.
$$

Use the normal displacement jump for free surface charge. The conductor normal displacement vanishes with its normal electric field.

$$
\sigma_f=D_{n,\rm diel}-D_{n,\rm con}=D\cos\alpha.
$$

Tangential electric field is continuous. Obtain it from the dielectric displacement and use conductor Ohm law for the tangential current.

$$
E_{t,\rm con}=E_{t,\rm diel}=\frac{D\sin\alpha}{\varepsilon_0\varepsilon},\qquad\mathbf j_{\rm con}=\frac{D\sin\alpha}{\rho\varepsilon_0\varepsilon}\mathbf t,\qquad j_n=0.
$$

**Results**

Free surface charge density in the dc steady state

$$
\sigma_f=D\cos\alpha
$$

Tangential conductor current density at the boundary

$$
\mathbf j=\frac{D\sin\alpha}{\rho\varepsilon_0\varepsilon}\mathbf t,\quad j_n=0
$$

**Limitations**

- During charging transients, normal current equals the surface-charge accumulation rate and is not determined by the supplied D and angle alone.
- A leaky exterior dielectric would require its conductivity and could carry a nonzero normal current.

## 3.165

**Solved · high confidence.** Use local Ohm law and an interfacial Gauss-law pillbox, distinguishing total from free charge.

j=I/S,\qquad E_1=\rho_1I/S,\quad E_2=\rho_2I/S.
